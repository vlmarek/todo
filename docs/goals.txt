TODO PROJECT GOALS
==================

Know what needs attention. Show what got done.

todo helps one person manage daily work from the terminal, using Todoist
for task storage, phone and web access, and reminder delivery. It should
reduce the effort of deciding what to do, maintaining work, and reporting
progress. These goals guide product and implementation choices; the
accepted specification defines exact behavior and scope.

- Make the next action clear. Show actionable tasks and steps in a useful
  urgency order, with enough context to understand them. Keep intentionally
  deferred work out of the normal queue and bring it back according to
  its schedule. Prioritize avoiding missed urgent work over perfect filtering.

- Reduce the effort of maintaining work. Make capture, inspection, progress
  notes, scheduling, completion and ordinary changes direct and consistent.
  Teach clear canonical commands, keep help useful, and avoid making the
  user understand internal storage or synchronization machinery.

- Keep progress visible. Support comments and completed steps as meaningful
  progress before a whole task is finished. Preserve their relationship
  to the task so reports explain both completed work and ongoing effort.

- Make reporting trustworthy. Include eligible completions and progress
  within the requested interval. Preserve known unreported work across
  refreshes and cache rebuilding. Advance the report boundary only after
  complete final output succeeds; never silently skip work because required
  information could not be retrieved.

- Change the intended item. Resolve ambiguity explicitly, keep identity
  stable through renames and moves, and show the scope and consequences
  of changes where confirmation is required. Do not guess the user's target
  or silently repair unrelated state.

- Respect scheduling intent. Keep attention dates, recurrence, hiding and
  reminders coherent. Handle account timezones and explicit input zones
  consistently, explain unsupported or ambiguous times, and show the actual
  accepted schedule rather than merely repeating the requested expression.

- Work alongside Todoist. Treat Todoist as authoritative for remote work
  and accept supported changes made through its other clients. Validate
  information needed for the managed workflow without rejecting harmless
  external metadata or rewriting data merely to enforce CLI conventions.
  Keep unrelated account data outside the application's retained scope.

- Keep everyday interaction responsive. Use saved state for ordinary reads,
  make refresh explicit, and reuse sufficient retrieved evidence. Avoid
  redundant requests and unrelated historical retrieval while preserving
  verification of accepted changes and completeness of reports.

- Tell the truth about outcomes. Distinguish successful changes, rejected
  changes, uncertain remote outcomes and failures to save local state.
  Explain what is known, what remains uncertain and the applicable next
  step. Keep routine successful output concise and failures actionable.

- Protect user data and control. Preserve required history, credentials,
  account binding and report boundaries. Keep disposable cache separate
  from information that cannot simply be fetched again. Never present
  incomplete or invalid state as an empty workload or a complete report.

- Keep the product focused and maintainable. Favor the smallest coherent
  solution for this personal terminal workflow. Share domain rules and
  command behavior, keep installation and recovery understandable, and
  avoid speculative frameworks or features without a concrete user need.

- Verify meaningful behavior. Test complete user workflows and consequential
  failures, including selection, external changes, scheduling, partial
  mutations and report finalization. Keep automated verification isolated
  from real accounts, and distinguish simulated evidence from behavior
  actually observed against Todoist.
